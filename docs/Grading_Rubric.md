# Grading Rubric

| Category | Unacceptable (0–59%) | Acceptable (60–89%) | Excellent (90%+) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Functionality (35%)** | Fails to handle order of operations. Fails on keywords used as identifiers when it is legal to do so. Scanner crashes on valid input. AST structure is flat or missing branches. | Correctly parses all statements and arithmetic including routine calls. May have minor bugs in complex nested expressions. | Flawless AST generation. Handles edge cases with mathematical precision, including keywords as identifiers and 3-value logic. |
| **Video: Understanding & Logic (25%)** | Cannot explain the mapping from BNF rule to Racket function. Confuses lexing and parsing. | Explains how lexer & parser interact, and how keywords as identifiers are handled. Traces through a loop or routine call. | Explains how the grammar hierarchy enforces operator precedence and left-associativity. |
| **Video: Reflection & Analysis (15%)** | No mention of formal grammars or ASTs. Treats the project as a simple string-manipulation exercise. | Discusses why recursive descent is easier in Racket than in C/Java. | Critiques design of grammar, including keyword/identifier issue. Discusses LL(1) constraints and transition from concrete syntax to abstract representation. |
| **Documentation: AI Prompt Logs (15%)** | No logs provided, or logs show "blind copying" of logic without any iterative refinement or questioning. | Logs show use of AI for Racket syntax and debugging. Evidence of the student verifying AI-generated logic. Evidence of iterative process of refinement. | Logs demonstrate a "Thought Partner" approach. AI was used to explore grammar alternatives or to refactor code for better readability. |
| **Code Quality & Coding Style (10%)** | Variables like x, y, lst used everywhere. No modularity. Code is a single monolithic function. | Follows Racket naming conventions. Functions are clearly mapped to BNF non-terminals. Proper indentation and spacing. | Elegant use of match. Functions are concise and "Self-Documenting." Code reads like a formal specification of the grammar. |
